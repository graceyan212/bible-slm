# Design Research — Kids' Bible App (ages 7–9)

Research gathered to inform the **design system** (visual direction, typography, color, gamification,
and the developmental/faith-formation science behind the reward design) for the SBC kids' Bible guide.

Product context: a warm, tiered SBC Bible **guide** for ages 7–9 that *retells* stories (never quotes
Scripture verbatim), **holds** closed-hand doctrine, and **deflects** family-owned questions to parents.
Design tone chosen: **Adventure & wonder**. Structural direction under discussion: a unified
**"atlas-journal"** — a treasure-map journey (navigation + progress) bound into an explorer's journal
(badge/sticker collection + a un-gamified "Wonderings" page that captures deflected questions for a parent).

## Headline takeaways for the design system

1. **Two visual worlds exist:** the "trusted-kid" look (flat, rounded, saturated, one bold mascot — Duolingo/Khan) vs. the "cool" look (blocky/cinematic 3D worlds — Roblox/Minecraft). For a faith app that must earn parent trust while feeling adventurous, the **warm storybook/painterly 2D** direction is both on-brand and the most feasible.
2. **The proven mechanics for 7–9:** world-map/path progression, collectible badges/stickers, a companion mascot, and progress bars. Peak "collecting age" (Piaget concrete-operational).
3. **The one rule that governs everything:** *keep the sacred un-gamified.* Overjustification research (worse in children) says reward **showing up and effort**, never the wondering/prayer/faith itself.
4. **Feasibility:** hand-painted/flat 2D (optionally AI-assisted with a custom-trained LoRA for character consistency) is by far the most achievable for a small team; full 3D Pixar-style is not.
5. **Compliance:** design to the UK Children's Code / CA AADC — no manipulative streaks, FOMO pushes, variable-ratio "loot" loops, or child-vs-child leaderboards.
