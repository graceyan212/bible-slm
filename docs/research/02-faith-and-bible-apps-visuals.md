# Faith / Bible Apps & Media for Kids: Visual Style

Scope note: two originally-named targets deviate from the "children's app" frame — **Bibly** is a
minimalist *adult* Bible reader (not a kids' product), and **Gracelink** is a print/PowerPoint
Sunday-school curriculum (no app, no gamification). Reported as they actually are.

---

## YouVersion — Bible App for Kids (Life.Church + OneHope) — closest direct genre comp

- **Visual:** Hand-illustrated **storybook aesthetic with touch-activated 2D animation**; stories broken
  into small tappable scenes. Marketing stresses "beautiful animations" and "colorful illustrations."
- **Color:** Bright, vibrant, high-saturation, tuned to hold young attention.
- **Character design:** Simplified, approachable, friendly cartoon renderings (Creation, Exodus, Jesus)
  — stylized rather than realistic, for pre-/early readers. Art produced by animation studio **Timbuktoons**.
- **Gamification (strong):** tap-to-animate objects; read-aloud narration + subtitles; collectible
  **"Bible gems"** unlock new story collections; profile **badges** (trophy icon); collectible **"fruits
  of the Spirit"**; up to **three stars** per level; **daily login streak**; end-of-lesson quizzes.
  Usable independently by kids as young as 3–4. 41 core stories, 70+ languages, ad-free, no IAP, offline.
- Sources: https://www.youversion.com/bible-app-for-kids · https://onehope.net/global-outreach/what-we-do/the-bible-app-for-kids/ · https://timbuktoons.com/work/bibleapp

## Bibly (adult Bible reader — reality check)

- Not a kids app, not animated. Free iOS Bible reading app for a general/adult audience by Christian
  designer Chris Lee. **Minimal, clean, text-forward** — typography + whitespace, no character art.
  Features (not gamification): AI verse explanations, reflection prompts, cross-references.
- Sources: https://faith.tools/bible-study

## Gracelink (Seventh-day Adventist children's Sabbath School curriculum)

- Print + PowerPoint 12-year curriculum (624 lessons); **not an app.** A revision deliberately
  "returned to a more traditional graphics style"; Junior/Teen illustrations are **"true-to-life,
  rather than comic format"** (realistic depiction vs. cartoon). Being superseded by "Alive in Jesus."
- Sources: https://www.outlookmag.org/gracelink-sabbath-school-curriculum-revised/ · https://www.gracelink.net/

## RightNow Media Kids

- Not one style — a curated **catalog** (2,000+ videos) spanning live-action, 2D animation, 3D CGI, and
  comic-book art; quality explicitly variable. Representative: *Bibleman* (superhero), *Owlegories*
  (bright 2D nature cartoon), *Ryan Defrates: Secret Agent* (action), a redemption series drawn by
  "Marvel and DC artists" (comic-book style), *The Creators* (flagship live-action). Also carries Superbook.
- Sources: https://www.rightnowmedia.org/kids

## Minno (formerly JellyTelly)

- A streaming **aggregator** (2,300+ episodes / 130 shows), no unified house style: *VeggieTales*
  (3D CGI vegetables), *321 Penguins* (CGI), *Owlegories* (2D + songs), *Young David* (2025, cinematic
  action). Draws creators from PBS KIDS, Nickelodeon, Cartoon Network — "mainstream quality." Unified by
  family-friendly tone, not a consistent look.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minno · https://gominno.com/

## Superbook (CBN) — the reference point, and a natural style experiment

### Classic (1981–83) — Japanese anime

- **Visual:** hand-drawn **anime** by **Tatsunoko Production** (Speed Racer, Samurai Pizza Cats) with
  CBN. Its look was "distinctly different from American animation," and was many Western/Filipino
  viewers' first exposure to anime.
- **Premise/characters:** the **Superbook is a physical talking ancient book** that time-travels kids
  Chris and Joy into Bible events. **Gizmo** — a wind-up clockwork robot designed to evoke a
  Crusader/Templar knight (golden cross on the chest), "speaks" via a flashing nose.
- **Why kids remembered it:** novel anime aesthetic; time-travel adventure premise; massive reach
  (500M+ viewers, 52 episodes, 43 languages, 106 countries).

### Reboot (2011–21) — 3D CGI

- **Visual:** fully **3D CGI** ("state-of-the-art"), anime-influenced via Asia-Pacific production
  partners. Crafted with **Emmy-winning artists whose credits include The Lion King, Beauty and the
  Beast, Mulan** (directors incl. Tom & Tony Bancroft).
- **Key change:** the Superbook is reimagined as a **handheld glowing electronic device** (not a
  physical book). More cinematic/mature (David & the lion; Jesus' scars) while staying kid-appropriate.
- **Why kids love it:** exciting time-travel adventure; relatable modern kids + Gizmo; high-quality CGI
  that "still holds up"; clear moral lessons.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superbook_(1981_TV_series) · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superbook_(2011_TV_series) · https://secure.cbn.com/superbook/series-history.aspx

> **Design implication:** Superbook's durable hook across *both* eras was **"a magical book that takes
> you places" + time-travel adventure**, not the render style. That directly supports making our app's
> container itself a **book/atlas-journal** — the emotional core of the #1 inspiration, achievable in 2D.

## Cross-cutting takeaways for a 7–9 faith audience

- Two illustration philosophies: **stylized/cartoon** (YouVersion, most animation, Superbook) vs.
  **true-to-life realism** (Gracelink Junior). The kid-app winners lean **stylized + bright + saturated**.
- **Gamification lives in the apps** — YouVersion is the clearest exemplar (gems, badges, stars,
  streaks, tap-to-animate, quizzes). Streaming platforms and curricula have little/no game layer.
- **The Action Bible** (art by Sergio Cariello, a Marvel/DC veteran) is bold, dynamic comic-book line
  art — the strongest reference for the "cool," boy-appealing top end of 7–9 (see file 04).
