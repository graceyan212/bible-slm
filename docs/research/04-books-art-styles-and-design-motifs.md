# Illustrated Books (ages 7–9): Art Styles + Validated Design Motifs

---

## Part A — Chapter/graphic-book art styles that resonate at 7–9

Ordered simplest → most cinematic.

- **The Bad Guys (Aaron Blabey)** — simplest, gag-driven. **Greyscale only** (black/white/grey), pen +
  Copic markers; loose, sketchy, highly expressive; oversized dynamic typography for emotion/motion.
  Color deliberately omitted — great for reluctant readers.
  Sources: https://www.howtolovecomics.com/2022/06/01/the-bad-guys-graphic-novel-series/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bad_Guys_(book_series)
- **Dog Man (Dav Pilkey)** — deliberately **"kid-drawn"** cartoon. Simple, loose, intentionally
  unpolished linework ("I could draw this too"); bright, bold, high-saturation primary color;
  exaggerated expressions, rounded forms. Lowers the intimidation barrier; the fiction frames the comics
  as made by kid characters, mirroring the reader.
  Sources: https://pilkey.com/series/dog-man
- **Wings of Fire (graphic novels; line: Mike Holmes, color: Maarta Laiho)** — detailed, fairly fine
  linework, still cartoon not realistic; full rich color by a dedicated colorist; distinctive "kid-like"
  dragon designs fans actively copy (participation-inviting), and consistent enough to be recognizable.
  Sources: https://firstsecondbooks.com/behind-the-panels/behind-the-panels-mike-holmes/
- **Percy Jackson (graphic novels)** — traditional Western adventure-comic style, full color,
  conventional paneling.
- **The Action Bible (Sergio Cariello)** — mainstream **superhero-comic** style. Bold energetic inking,
  dynamic action poses, rich saturated cinematic coloring; biblical figures rendered like heroic
  protagonists. Cariello is a Marvel/DC veteran (Batman, Spider-Man) who deliberately replaced the older
  *Picture Bible*'s "calm" blocks with "more dynamic, more emotion." Treats sacred narrative with the
  visual grammar kids already associate with heroes. (Publishers market it ages 9–12 — the top of our band.)
  Sources: https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/authors/interviews/article/100517-sergio-cariello-man-of-action.html · https://familyfiction.com/action-bible-illustrated-by-marvel-and-dc-comics-artist-sergio-cariello/
- **Chronicles of Narnia (Pauline Baynes)** — pen-and-ink; fluid gestural line + tight textural detail;
  masterful negative space; decorative (Persian-miniature influence). Originally line-only; later a
  watercolor-tint edition. Baynes also drew the **Narnia maps** (wartime Admiralty chart-making
  background) — *a direct reference for a warm, hand-inked treasure-map in a storybook idiom.*
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_Baynes · https://booksforkeeps.co.uk/article/illustrating-narnia/
- **Amulet (Kazu Kibuishi)** — most painterly/cinematic. Manga + Western illustration; soft rounded
  friendly faces over atmospheric, mood-soaked environments ("Final Fantasy + Lord of the Rings +
  Studio Ghibli").
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amulet_(comics)

**Cross-cutting:** proven 7–9 styles cluster around (a) **bold confident outlines**, (b) **exaggerated,
emotionally legible faces**, (c) **either high-saturation primary color OR a confident limited/greyscale
palette**, and (d) an **"imitable," non-intimidating** quality that invites the child in. Painterly
realism (Amulet) works but sits at the atmospheric end; the mainstream sweet spot is **cartoon line +
bold color.**

## Part B — Design motifs, ranked by evidence for ages 7–9

Developmental frame: 7–9 is Piaget's **concrete-operational** stage (logic on tangible things) + the
peak of **collecting/sorting/completing**. NN/g cautions kids take instructions literally, need clear
specific goals, and shouldn't be treated as one age group (a 7-yo ≠ a 9-yo).

1. **Companion character / mascot — deepest research base.** Children form **parasocial relationships**
   (enduring one-sided bonds) with characters, and these measurably **improve learning** (e.g., toddlers
   learned math better from a character they'd bonded with via a plush toy; parasocial bond + "math
   talk" predicted faster, more accurate responses + better transfer). Bond-building principles: social
   contingency (timely, personalized response — Dora's pause), invite participation / break the 4th wall,
   transmedia presence (physical toys), involve parents, design for attachment. Duo the Owl is the
   commercial exemplar.
   Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7818392/ · https://cdmc.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Brunick-et-al-2016.pdf
2. **Collectible badges / stickers / sets — strongly supported.** Set-collection is a documented
   *hallmark* of middle childhood, driven by classification + goal-planning (tracking have-vs-need). A
   study of 6–9 year-olds found gamification (cover story + feedback + points + reward box) raised task
   completion; a 2025 K–12 meta-analysis (31 studies) found a moderate-to-large effect on motivation
   (g = 0.654). **Caveat:** effects are mostly *extrinsic*; **context-relevant** rewards beat generic ones.
   Sources: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK216782/ · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096524003096 · https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pits.70056
3. **World-map / level-path progression — strongly supported.** Converts abstract progress into a
   visible, tangible journey; "the player always knows where she is and the next action." Fits the
   concrete-operational preference for tangible goals. **Ethical caveat:** Candy Crush weaponizes
   sunk-cost + intermittent reward + "pinch levels" for monetization; **Duolingo is the better model**
   (measures learning quality, offers opt-outs) for a kids' product.
   Sources: https://cjleo.com/blog/1001-levels-of-candy-crush/ · https://www.thepmrepo.com/articles/how-duolingo-gamified-monthly-active-users-lessons-in-habit-formation
4. **Quests / narrative structure — supported, with a caveat.** Narrative is intrinsically motivating
   ("what happens next?") and improves memory/engagement; Quest Atlantis (~age 9) produced large
   pre/post gains via "transformational play" (child as protagonist whose choices matter). **Caveat:**
   the story must be **intrinsically integrated** with the learning goal, not decoration.
   Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.13749
5. **Treasure maps / journals — supported by inference, thin direct evidence.** No single study isolates
   these motifs for 7–9, but they're a **thematic container** for the well-evidenced mechanics above
   (map = visible progression; journal = the collecting/recording drive; diary-format books like *Diary
   of a Wimpy Kid* are hugely successful with this band). **Use map + journal as delivery vehicles** for
   progression + collecting rather than as standalone proven mechanics.

**Art-direction bridge:** the **Pauline Baynes Narnia maps** show the treasure-map motif executed in a
warm, hand-inked children's-book idiom — a model for making a progression map feel *storybook* rather
than *video-game HUD*.

Sources (motifs): https://www.nngroup.com/articles/kids-cognition/ · https://www.nngroup.com/reports/children-on-the-web/
