# Popular Video Games (ages 7–9): Art, Engagement & "Cool"

Covers the broader video-game landscape (beyond Roblox/Minecraft/Pokémon, which are in file 01) to
understand what "cool" looks like in games and which mechanics are ethical to borrow for a calm faith app.

---

## Part 1 — The games

### Fortnite (Epic)
- **Art:** Bright, stylized 3D, "Pixar-adjacent" semi-realism; saturated, clean readable silhouettes;
  deliberately colorful vs. gritty military shooters.
- **Loop:** The **Battle Pass** (~100-tier seasonal track, ~10–12 wk seasons) is the engine; **V-Bucks**
  buy skins/emotes/gliders from a rotating limited-time **Item Shop**; one-time **live events**
  (concerts, crossovers). Cosmetics are purely aesthetic (no pay-to-win) — self-expression is the appeal.
- **Why cool:** social hangout; skins as **identity/status** ("playground currency"); constant novelty;
  dance emotes as meme fuel; "the game older kids play."
- **Concerns:** ESRB **Teen 13+**; it *is* a shooter; Common Sense flags **FOMO monetization**, open
  voice chat, spend pressure. Sources: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/fortnite · https://www.esrb.org/ratings/33643/fortnite/

### Nintendo Switch first-party
- **Super Mario Odyssey / Wonder** — polished 3D themed "kingdoms" (Odyssey) / hand-drawn expressive
  2D "living cartoon" (Wonder); iconic rounded mascot. **Loop:** abundant, low-stakes **collection**
  (Power Moons / Wonder Seeds) + power-ups; no monetization. **Appeal:** forgiving, joyful discovery,
  "one more moon" pull, visible mastery.
- **Mario Kart** — clean bright 3D. **Loop:** race + **catch-up items** (blue shell) keep weaker players
  competitive; unlock karts/characters. **Appeal:** a 7-yo can beat a parent — huge for confidence.
- **Zelda: BOTW / TOTK** — painterly **cel-shaded** open world, muted natural palette + bloom; "wonder
  of exploration" is the entire aesthetic thesis. **Loop:** open-ended exploration, bite-size shrine
  puzzles, Korok collection. Skews older but is **the gold-standard wonder/exploration tone.** E10+.
- **Splatoon 2/3** — **neon, ink-splattered graffiti/streetwear** youth aesthetic; ink-covering (not
  shooting people) is the core metaphor. **Loop:** Turf War, **Splatfests** (limited-time community
  team events), heavy fashion/cosmetic customization. **Appeal:** "cool without being violent." E10+.
- **Animal Crossing: New Horizons** *(key model, see Part 2)* — soft rounded pastel "cozy" 3D. **Loop:**
  **real-time clock/calendar**, gentle daily tasks, **collection** (bugs/fish/fossils → museum),
  decorating; **no fail state, no timers, no combat.** The definitive **non-competitive** hit.
- **Kirby** — ultra-round pink hero, candy-soft palette, maximum cute; **easiest on-ramp for young kids**
  (forgiving, can fly, never punishing).
- **Luigi's Mansion 3** — "spooky-but-cute," comedic ghosts, drop-in co-op.
- **Smash Bros. Ultimate** — high-gloss crossover fan-service; roster + Spirits as a collection; party play.
- Sources: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/super-mario-odyssey · https://www.zelda.com/tears-of-the-kingdom/ · https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/animal-crossing-new-horizons · https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/splatoon-3

### LEGO video games (TT Games)
- **Art:** everything built from **LEGO bricks** in 3D; smashable into studs; bright, toyetic; IP rebuilt
  in brick form.
- **Loop:** smash → collect **studs** → unlock hundreds of characters/vehicles; hidden minikits; "True
  Jedi/100%" completion; **silent slapstick humor**; **no life-loss penalty** (characters re-form).
- **Appeal:** drop-in/drop-out **couch co-op** (great with a parent), gentle universal humor, massive
  collectibility, essentially no fail-state stress. **Ideal 7–9 title.** E10+.
- Source: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/lego-star-wars-the-skywalker-saga

### Sonic
- Vivid high-saturation; blue mascot with attitude; classic 2D + colorful 3D. **Loop:** **speed** as
  fantasy; rings = a forgiving health buffer. **Appeal:** exhilaration + "cool mascot," boosted by the
  hit movies with this exact age. E10+.

### Among Us
- **Minimalist flat 2D**; the **bean-shaped crewmate** is a meme-able, endlessly customizable design
  icon. **Loop:** social deduction; cosmetics economy (hats/skins/pets, free + paid). **Appeal:** social
  play + deception, tiny download, beloved meme. Cartoon "killing" → rated ~10+.
- Source: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/among-us

### Stardew Valley (cozy life-sim) *(key model, see Part 2)*
- **Art:** warm **16-bit pixel art**, seasonal color shifts. **Loop:** farm across a **four-season
  calendar**; **collection** (museum / community-center bundles); **no fail state**, self-paced; days end
  gently. The **cozy genre** (Animal Crossing, Stardew, Spiritfarer, Cozy Grove, Palia, *Hello Kitty
  Island Adventure*) = no/low fail states, calm pacing, collection-forward loops, seasonal rhythm, décor.
- Sources: https://www.stardewvalley.net/ · https://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/stardew-valley

### Mobile / casual
- **Subway Surfers** — bright cel-shaded 3D, graffiti palette; endless runner; coins → cosmetic
  characters/boards; daily challenges, themed seasons, mystery boxes. Famous as TikTok "sludge content"
  background video → huge brand familiarity with young kids. ~9+.
- **Candy Crush** — glossy hyper-saturated; match-3 on a long **level map**; **lives/energy timer** (soft
  gate) + boosters + difficulty spikes to push spending. *Engagement-pressure patterns to avoid.*
- **Angry Birds** — clean cartoon 2D, big-eyed characters; physics puzzles, 3-star completion.
- **Brawl Stars** — bright top-down 3D; collect 80+ Brawlers; **battle pass**, skins, **trophies/ranking**
  (historically loot-box "Boxes," since reworked after criticism). E10+.
- **Clash Royale / Clash of Clans** — card collection + upgrade, **chests** (loot-box-style), ladder
  ranking, heavy monetization — the **least appropriate models** here.
- **Just Dance** — neon motion-graphics; mirror choreography to licensed pop; family living-room play.
- Sources: https://sybogames.com/ · https://www.king.com/game/candycrush · https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/

*Popularity evidence:* Ofcom **Children's Media Use & Attitudes** (https://www.ofcom.org.uk/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/childrens) · Common Sense **Census** (https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research).

## Part 2 — Synthesis

### 1. What "cool" looks like in games vs. the "cute educational-app" look
Games kids love share: **high saturation + bold confident palettes** (not pastel-washed); **strong
readable silhouettes / iconic mascots** (recognizable as a black shape — Kirby's circle, the Among Us
bean, Sonic, Mario — *the single most transferable principle*); **depth + "production value"** (dynamic
lighting, particle "juice," satisfying motion → reads as "for me, the big-kid stuff"); **stylization over
realism** (cel-shaded/painterly/voxel — never uncanny, never flat-and-plain); **expressiveness + humor.**

The **educational-app look** (rounded sans, pastel, wide-eyed mascot, flat vector, primary blocks) reads
to a 7–9-yo as **"younger than me / homework."** The gap to close: keep warmth and safety, but add
**saturation, depth, iconic silhouettes, and polish** so the app feels like a *real adventure product*.
For this age, **"cool" ≠ edgy** — it = **vivid, polished, expressive, treated like the big games** —
which is fully compatible with reverence.

### 2. Ethical to borrow vs. manipulative to avoid
**Borrow (healthy):**
- **Collection for its own sake** with *deterministic, known* rewards (Animal Crossing museum, Stardew
  bundles, LEGO characters) — no randomness, no purchase.
- **Mastery & visible progression** (level a skill, fill a map, earn a badge for demonstrated learning).
- **Cosmetic self-expression without pay pressure** (customize an avatar/space, all earned free).
- **Gentle daily/seasonal rhythm** that is *invitational, not punishing*.
- **Forgiving / catch-up / no-fail design** (Mario Kart items, Sonic rings, Kirby flight, LEGO re-forming).
- **Co-op over competition** (LEGO, Luigi's Mansion) — builds warmth, not rivalry.

**Avoid (manipulative — especially for a children's faith app):**
- **Loot boxes / randomized paid rewards** (Clash chests) — gambling-adjacent for minors. *Hard no.*
- **FOMO limited-time battle passes / rotating shops** (Fortnite) — engineered scarcity/urgency.
- **Variable-ratio / variable-reward loops** — the slot-machine schedule; use **fixed, earned,
  predictable** rewards instead.
- **Streak-pressure / loss-framed dailies** — guilt/anxiety; especially wrong in a faith context.
- **Competitive ranking / leaderboards** — comparison + status anxiety; antithetical to the tone.
- **Energy/lives timers + pay-to-continue** (Candy Crush).
- Grounding: ICO Children's Code (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/) · FTC dark patterns (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/protecting-consumers/dark-patterns) · WHO gaming disorder.

### 3. Cozy games as the north star
For a **calm faith app**, the cozy genre — not the competitive shooter — is the right model:
- **No fail states.** Progress only accumulates; you cannot "lose" → protects a contemplative mood.
- **Player-paced, not app-paced.** The child decides how much; the app never punishes absence (Animal
  Crossing *invites* return via seasons; it doesn't threaten).
- **Collection is the heartbeat** — a museum/journal that visibly fills; deterministic, earned,
  browsable, proud to show.
- **Seasonal/real-time rhythm** = gentle freshness (a "creation" theme in spring, Advent in December)
  without FOMO — missing it isn't a loss; it comes around again.
- **Ownership of a personal space** → the child's own journal/garden they cultivate.
- **Tactile, satisfying micro-feedback** applied to gentle actions, not dopamine-slot loops.

### 4. Borrowable design ideas for the 7–9 Bible app
(Adventure-&-wonder tone; treasure-map/journal structure; badge collection; **sacred kept un-gamified**.)

1. **A treasure-map "world" you explore** (Zelda/Mario-kingdom): regions unlock as the child journeys;
   each location is a story. Borrow BOTW's warm, luminous, painterly exploration palette — **the map is
   the progress bar** (no percentages needed).
2. **A collectible "journal / museum of wonders"** (Animal Crossing museum × Stardew bundles): collect
   **story keepsakes / illustrated moments** — deterministic, earned by *engaging with a story*, never
   random, never bought; browsable and beautiful to revisit. The primary collection loop.
3. **Earned badges tied to genuine engagement/mastery, not grinding** — a milestone token given once and
   kept forever; no tiers to grind, no leaderboard, no comparison.
4. **Iconic mascot/companion with a strong silhouette** — a gentle guide for the journey; expressive,
   warm, recognizable as a shape ("cool character" energy without competition).
5. **Avatar / personal-space self-expression, all free and earned** — customize a companion or the
   journal/garden with collected items (the *healthy* half of Fortnite/Splatoon appeal, zero purchase/FOMO).
6. **Gentle seasonal rhythm, invitational not streak-based** — content following the real/liturgical
   calendar (Advent, Easter) appears as a *welcome*, never a "you missed a day."
7. **Forgiving, no-fail interaction** — like LEGO's re-forming characters / Sonic's rings; a child can
   never "fail" a story; mistakes are frictionless; tone stays safe and confident.
8. **Keep the sacred deliberately un-gamified — and make that a feature.** Hard line: *stories, Scripture,
   prayer/wonder earn no points, badges, or currency.* Reward the *journey around* the sacred (exploring,
   collecting keepsakes, returning); present the sacred reverently, full-screen, uninterrupted, with no
   reward animation. **The ethical inverse of a loot box: the most valuable thing in the app is the thing
   you deliberately don't gamify.** (Fits the product's retell-not-quote + wondering-to-parent principles.)

## Part 3 — Addendum: current (2025–26) popularity data + gamification ethics evidence

### What kids actually play right now
- **89% of UK children 3–17 play games**; gaming is the single top free-time activity for 8–17s (Ofcom,
  *Children and Parents: Media Use and Attitudes 2025*). **97% of 8–17s play online games.** **83% of
  Gen Alpha (5–12) play weekly** (ESA).
- **US kids age ≤8 now average 38 min/day gaming — up 65% in five years** (5–8s went 40→64 min/day); most
  of it on phones/tablets (Common Sense **Census 2025, *Media Use by Kids Zero to Eight***).
- Dominant franchises for 6–13s: **Roblox, Minecraft, Fortnite**, then Mario Kart, EA Sports FC, Super
  Mario, Brawl Stars. Scale: Roblox ~380M MAU / ~85M DAU (2025); Fortnite ~650M registered.
- **2025–26 breakout hits — the key new signal:** both are **low-stress, non-combat, collection/creation**
  Roblox games. **Grow a Garden** (a "chill Animal Crossing vibe" farming sim) peaked at **~21.6M
  concurrent** (July 2025), beating Fortnite's all-time peak; **Steal a Brainrot** (TikTok-meme universe)
  hit **~25.8M concurrent** (Oct 2025) — reportedly the highest ever for any game. *The biggest current
  hits with this exact age skew cozy/collection, not competitive shooter* — aligning with our direction.
- Sources: https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-children/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025 · https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-2025-common-sense-census-media-use-by-kids-zero-to-eight · https://fortune.com/2025/07/30/roblox-video-game-grow-a-garden-viral-summer-hit/ · https://www.theesa.com/annual-esa-study-reveals-video-games-universal-appeal-across-generations/

### Gamification ethics — the harder evidence
- **Loot boxes ↔ problem gambling:** Spicer et al. (2022) systematic review — **12 of 13 studies found a
  positive link** (mean r ≈ .27; loot-box↔problem-gaming r ≈ .40). **56% of mobile games with loot boxes
  are rated 7+**, a core child-exposure concern. *Hard no for our product.*
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448211027175 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8794181/
- **FTC dark patterns** (*Bringing Dark Patterns to Light*, 2022) explicitly cites children's apps and the
  tactic where "a button a child repeatedly taps to advance a level is suddenly turned into a 'Buy'
  button." FTC v. Epic/Fortnite → **$275M COPPA penalty** (largest ever).
  https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800+Dark+Patterns+Report+9.14.2022+-+FINAL.pdf
- **ICO Children's Code** specifically targets **"sticky features" — reward loops, continuous scroll,
  notifications, auto-play — designed to extend engagement**, and bars nudge techniques detrimental to
  wellbeing. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/audits/data-protection-audit-framework/toolkits/age-appropriate-design/nudge-techniques/
- **Streaks:** design analysis warns they cross "a threshold where engagement becomes obligation" via loss
  aversion; **ethical streak design** (Duolingo cited) = serve real user goals, **collaboration over
  competition, compassionate non-shaming language, tally/cumulative rather than all-or-nothing** framing.
- **WHO gaming disorder** (ICD-11): worldwide problematic-gaming ~1–2%; **children are especially prone
  because cognitive control is still developing.**
- **The design test** synthesized from this literature: *Does the feature help the child reach a goal they
  value, or manufacture obligation/fear to drive metrics?*

### Visual design is AGE-DRIVEN (important nuance for our 7–9 band)
- **Ages ~2–7:** rounded/pastel/friendly-mascot look strongly favored; mascots double as narrators.
- **Ages ~8+/tweens:** the pastel-mascot look **reads as "babyish" and is often rejected — sometimes
  permanently**; tweens prefer "grown-up," game-adjacent UI (clean lines, deeper/muted palettes, modern
  type, Discord/Twitch-adjacent). Younger kids like bright primaries; older kids like deeper muted tones.
- **Implication for us:** 7–9 straddles this line. Lean warm/storybook for the 7-year-old, but add the
  **saturation, depth, iconic silhouettes, and polish** (Part 2 §1) that keep the 9-year-old from feeling
  it's "a baby app." Don't condescend — "don't mistake children's lack of cognitive development for a
  lack of sophistication."
- Sources: https://gapsystudio.com/blog/ux-design-for-kids/ · https://fruto.design/blog/digital-design-considerations-for-child-vs-adult-user-groups · https://www.aufaitux.com/blog/ui-ux-designing-for-children/

loot-box reviews. Tier 2 (journalistic, for the breakout-games narrative) — Fortune/ScreenRant; the exact
