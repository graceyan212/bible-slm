## A. Developmental psychology (Erikson, Piaget, social comparison)

- **Erikson — Industry vs. Inferiority** is Stage 4, ~ages 6–11 (Freud's latency period). Virtue =
  **"competence."** Motivation runs on **mastery, productivity, and recognition/approval**; **peer
  comparison is a central, defining mechanism**; without encouragement the child "begins to feel
  inferior, doubting his own abilities." Some inferiority is adaptive — balance is the goal. *(High
  confidence; Simply Psychology, tracing to Erikson.)*
- **Piaget — Concrete Operational (~7–11):**
  - **Conservation** (quantity persists despite appearance) passes ~age 7; emerges unevenly ("horizontal
    decalage": number → length → liquid → mass → weight).
  - **Classification / class inclusion** (a dog is both pet and animal; nested hierarchies) and
    **seriation** (order by a dimension) — the cognitive machinery behind collecting/sorting. A standard
    developmental-text passage: children's "interest in hierarchical classification becomes apparent in
    middle childhood when they begin to **collect items and spend hours sorting their collections**."
  - **Reversibility** (mentally undo an operation) underpins conservation and cause/effect reasoning.
  - **Decentration** (multiple aspects at once) → declining egocentrism → cooperative rule-based play.
  - Thinking is logical but **tied to concrete contexts**; abstract "what-if" reasoning waits for formal
    operations (~11–12). *(High confidence; the "rock collection" textbook passage was Medium — the
    open-textbook page was fetch-blocked but the quote recurs across sources.)*
- **Piaget — moral development (via the rules of marbles):** **heteronomous morality / moral realism
  (~5–9):** rules seen as external, sacred, unchangeable; acts judged by **consequences, not intention**;
  belief in "immanent justice." **Autonomous morality (~9–10):** rules are human agreements that can
  change; judge by **intention**; mutual respect. **7–9 is in transition** (outcome-based at 7–8,
  intention-based strengthening toward 9–10). *Contradiction:* Nelson (1980) found even 3-year-olds
  distinguish intention when tasks are simplified — Piaget underestimated the young. *(High confidence.)*
- **Social comparison & self-concept:** emerges ~**7–8** (Ruble; Livesly & Bromley 1973; McGuire &
  McGuire 1982 — "by age 8, children derive self-knowledge from social comparisons of all sorts"). A
  "mental/subjective self" and **internal standards** emerge ~age 8. Comparison is **double-edged**:
  upward comparisons can impair motivation/competence self-perception — a *developmentally new*
  vulnerability (preschoolers keep positive self-views even when out-performed). *(High confidence; NCBI
  Bookshelf NBK216782.)*

Sources: https://www.simplypsychology.org/erik-erikson.html · https://www.simplypsychology.org/concrete-operational.html · https://www.simplypsychology.org/piaget-moral.html · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK216782/

## B. Intrinsic/extrinsic motivation & Dweck (effect sizes)

- **Overjustification — Lepper, Greene & Nisbett (1973),** *JPSP* 28(1):129–137. 51 children, Bing
  Nursery School, Stanford. Expected-award group later drew ~**8.6%** of free time vs ~**16.7%** for
  no-award. **Boundary condition (often dropped):** the effect appears *only* when initial intrinsic
  interest is high **and** the reward is expected/salient. *(High confidence.)*
- **Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999),** *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6):627–668 (128 experiments):
  tangible expected rewards undermine free-choice intrinsic motivation — engagement-contingent
  **d ≈ −0.40**, completion-contingent **≈ −0.36**, performance-contingent **≈ −0.28**; self-reported
  interest also falls. **Positive feedback / verbal praise ENHANCES** it — free-choice **d ≈ +0.33**,
  interest **≈ +0.31**. **Children are more vulnerable than adults** (tangible rewards more detrimental;
  verbal praise less enhancing — kids separate informational from controlling aspects less easily).
  *(High for direction; Medium on exact decimals — scanned original.)*
- **Reward controversy (documented dispute):** Cameron & Pierce (1994) called the undermining effect
  minimal; DKR (1999) rebutted it as methodologically flawed. Behaviorist-leaning researchers still
  contest scope. *Weight of evidence supports real undermining for tangible/expected rewards.*
- **Cognitive Evaluation Theory (CET):** every reward has an **informational** aspect (→ internal locus
  → raises intrinsic motivation) and a **controlling** aspect (→ external locus → lowers it); whichever
  dominates determines the effect. *(High; Wikipedia CET was cleanly fetched.)*
- **Warneken & Tomasello:** 20-month-olds given a *material* reward for helping later helped **less** —
  even early prosocial behavior is intrinsically motivated and can be undermined. *(High for the finding;
  generalizing to 7–9 is an inference.)*
- **Mueller & Dweck (1998),** *JPSP* 75(1):33–52 (six studies, ~5th-graders): intelligence-praise →
  after failure, chose easier tasks, less persistence/enjoyment, ability attributions, **worse
  performance**, preference for performance over learning goals, fixed view of intelligence.
  Effort-praise → resilience, challenge-seeking. >1,600 citations. *(High; age skews ~10–11.)*
- **Kamins & Dweck (1999),** *Developmental Psychology* 35(3):835–847: **person/trait praise** ("you're
  so good") → contingent self-worth + helpless response after setbacks; **process praise** (effort/
  strategy) → resilient/mastery response — shown even in ~kindergarteners.
- **Gunderson et al. (2013),** *Child Development*: parents' **process praise** to toddlers predicted
  children's growth-mindset ~5 yrs later; **type mattered, not amount.** *(Medium on causal — longitudinal.)*
- **"False growth mindset" (Dweck's own caveat):** effort praise is "useless when the child is getting
  everything wrong and not making progress" — praise **process/strategies tied to real outcomes**, or it
  becomes hollow and signals low expectations.
- **Mindset intervention effect-size debate:** Sisk et al. (2018) — mindset↔achievement correlation ~1%
  of variance; intervention effect **d ≈ 0.08**. Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023, 122 studies) — near-null in
  best-designed studies. Yeager et al. (2019, *Nature*, ~12,500 9th-graders) — small real effect **only
  for lower-achievers in supportive contexts** (~0.10 GPA). **Scope note:** this debate is about
  *school-achievement interventions* (mostly adolescents), **not** the robust lab finding that
  *intelligence-praise makes kids more fragile than effort-praise* — don't conflate them.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overjustification_effect · https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/1999_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_evaluation_theory · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9686450/ · https://people.uncw.edu/hungerforda/infancy/pdf/person%20versus%20process%20praise%20and%20criticism.pdf · https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y · https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797617739704

## C. Named experts — detail behind the ranking

- **Eccles & Wigfield (Expectancy-Value / Situated EVT)** — *top developmental candidate.* Motivation =
  **expectancy of success × subjective task value** (attainment, intrinsic, utility, cost). Longitudinal
  work (Jacobs/Eccles/Wigfield 2002, grades 1–12) shows **competence beliefs and task values are highest
  in early elementary and decline steadily** — the decline **starts in the 7–9 window.** Explicitly
  developmental; the dominant framework. *(High.)*
- **Susan Harter** — self-worth is **multidimensional & hierarchical**: from middle childhood kids
  evaluate competence separately by domain (scholastic, social, athletic, appearance, conduct) plus a
  distinct **global self-worth** driven by the *discrepancy* between competence and how much the child
  *values* that domain. Her **Self-Perception Profile for Children (SPPC)** is validated for **ages
  8–12.** *(High, directly targeted.)*
- **Deci & Ryan (SDT)** — most rigorous general theory; strong child evidence (autonomy-supportive vs.
  controlling adults). Not 7–9-specific but robustly applicable; underpins Kohn and Dr. Becky.
- **Willingham** — "**Memory is the residue of thought**"; people are curious but **not** naturally good
  thinkers and avoid effortful thinking unless difficulty is pitched right (moderate, solvable =
  pleasurable); **"stories are psychologically privileged" in memory**; trying to make material
  "relevant to kids' interests" is less reliable than making it *meaningful*; "learning styles don't
  exist." *(High for cognition mechanisms.)*
- **Dweck** — descriptively applies to 7–9 (even young children hold coherent ability-vs-effort beliefs);
  intervention evidence stronger for adolescents/contested.
- **Kohn** — synthesizer/critic, not a primary researcher; borrows from Deci/Ryan; rebutted for
  caricaturing behaviorism (Reitman 1998).
- **Duckworth (grit)** — **weak fit for 7–9**; child scale (GSCA) starts grade 3; "consistency of
  interest" dimension is developmentally *inappropriate* (cycling interests is healthy at this age);
  Duckworth herself concedes the developmental critique; Credé et al. show grit ≈ conscientiousness.
- **Twenge** — teen/screen-time & generational; **off-target for 7–9** (screen-harm links larger for
  teens; younger kids mostly watch video, weaker link).
- **Dr. Becky Kennedy** — practitioner; "good inside," separate child from behavior, "behavior is a
  window," rejects sticker charts/rewards/time-outs; tools ("Most Generous Interpretation," "Two Things
  Are True"). Great for **parent-facing tone**; not primary evidence.

Sources: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0361476X99910159 · https://db.arabpsychology.com/scales/self-perception-profile-for-children-sppc/ · https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/theory/ · https://www.aft.org/ae/summer2009/willingham · https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691616673363

## D. Faith & moral development (Fowler, Kohlberg, Godly Play, Nye)

- **Fowler — Stage 2 "Mythic-Literal Faith"** (~6/7–11, aligns with concrete-operational):
  - Faith is **concrete, linear, rule-bound**; stories treated as **historical fact**, symbols taken
    **literally** (little allegory).
  - **Narrative is the primary vehicle** for meaning; children become "wonderful storytellers."
  - Strong belief in **reciprocal fairness / immanent justice** ("just world"); Fowler links this to
    Kohlberg's instrumental-exchange reasoning ("a concrete system of punishment and reward").
  - God conceived **anthropomorphically** (strict, just "old man in the sky"); symbols can slip into
    good-luck-charm function.
  - **Risk:** over-relying on strict reciprocity → when doing everything "right" still brings hardship
    (Job), the child may fall into "overcontrolling, stilted perfectionism," "an abasing sense of
    badness," or disillusionment. *(High confidence.)*
  - **Implication:** concrete narrative retelling lands; abstract/allegorical theology does not; **be
    cautious with strict "obey→reward" cosmic-justice framing** — it fits their instinct but sets up
    disillusionment.
- **Kohlberg — Preconventional (Level 1)** at 7–9 (straddling into early conventional): **Stage 1**
  obedience/punishment (rules fixed/absolute, judged by consequences, avoid punishment); **Stage 2**
  instrumental exchange (transactional reciprocity, "what's in it for me?"). **Critiques:** Gilligan
  (all-male sample; **ethic of care** vs. justice-only ladder — she did *not* claim care is female-only;
  later meta-analyses (Jaffee & Hyde 2000) find gender differences smaller than she claimed); cultural
  bias (Snarey 1985 — postconventional rare in some traditional societies). **Implication:** make
  consequences/fairness **concrete and relational**; don't assume kids act from internalized virtue;
  **foreground relationships/compassion**, not only rules/justice. *(High.)*
- **Godly Play (Jerome Berryman)** — Montessori/Catechesis-based, ~ages 3–12: storytelling → open-ended
  **"I wonder…"** questions → creative response. Wondering prompts are story-typed (sacred stories:
  identity — "where are you in this story?"; parables: symbolic — "what could this seed really be?").
  Facilitator is **explicitly non-judgmental** — "support the process of wondering, not to approve or
  disapprove of specific answers"; silence accepted; **no wrong answers**; "God is strong enough to speak
  to the child within the child's own wondering." Premise: children have innate spiritual capacity and
  "only lack the language." *(High; normative RE philosophy, not empirical.)*
- **Rebecca Nye** (*The Spirit of the Child* w/ David Hay; *Children's Spirituality*): children have a
  rich innate spiritual capacity "already there and something to be built on." Six conditions —
  **S.P.I.R.I.T.** (Space, Process, Imagination, Relationship, Intimacy, Trust) — emphasize unhurried
  space, imagination, relationship over content delivery; warns practices can "stimulate or mutilate"
  children's spirituality. *(Medium-High; the exact acronym seen in summary form.)*
- **"Sacred vs. earned" (design-critical):** attaching tangible rewards to spiritual engagement risks
  crowding out intrinsic/sacred motivation and reinforcing transactional theology; **worse in children.**
  Safer: **informational, autonomy-supportive feedback** ("here's what you noticed") over controlling
  rewards; reward **mechanical/skill** behaviors (reading fluency, showing up), **not the values/wondering
  itself.** Aligns with the Godly Play non-judgmental stance and the product's retell-not-quote +
  wondering-to-parent behavior.
